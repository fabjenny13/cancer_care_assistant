TOPIC: Eating when you have no appetite or feel nauseous
Treatment can change taste, reduce appetite and cause nausea, which makes eating feel like a chore. Small, frequent meals or snacks are often easier than three big ones. Cold or room-temperature foods smell less strongly and may be easier than hot ones. Plain foods such as toast, rice, banana, curd or soup are common choices on bad days, and sipping fluids through the day helps prevent dehydration. If metal tastes appear, plastic cutlery and adding lemon or herbs can help. Some people eat best at the time of day they feel best, not at mealtimes. Do not worry about a perfect diet during treatment; getting enough energy and fluids matters most. If you are losing weight, cannot keep fluids down or cannot eat for more than a day, tell the care team, and ask whether a dietitian can see you.
