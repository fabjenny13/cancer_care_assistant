TOPIC: When to contact your care team
Some symptoms during cancer treatment should not wait for the next appointment. Common reasons to call the care team promptly include a fever or chills, often a temperature of 38 degrees Celsius (100.4 Fahrenheit) or higher but follow the number your own team gave you, pain that is not controlled by the prescribed medicine, vomiting or diarrhoea that stops you keeping fluids down, unusual bleeding or bruising, new shortness of breath or chest pain, a painful swollen leg, confusion, or a wound or skin area that looks red, hot or is leaking. For severe breathing trouble, heavy bleeding, chest pain or fainting, go to the nearest emergency department. Keep your team's phone number somewhere easy to find, and do not worry about bothering them; checking early is what they would prefer.
