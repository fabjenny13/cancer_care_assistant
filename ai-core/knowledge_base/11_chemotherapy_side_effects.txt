TOPIC: Common chemotherapy side effects and what helps
Chemotherapy targets cells that divide quickly, which is why it can also affect healthy ones such as hair, the lining of the mouth and gut, and blood cells. Common effects include tiredness, nausea, hair thinning or loss, mouth sores, changes in taste, constipation or diarrhoea, and a lower blood count that makes infections more likely. Not everyone gets every effect, and they differ a lot between drugs and between people. Many can be eased. Anti-nausea medicines work best taken as prescribed, small frequent meals help, soft foods and gentle mouth care help with sores, and resting without guilt is part of treatment. A fever or chills during chemotherapy should be reported to the care team the same day, because low blood counts can make infections serious. The oncology team can explain what to expect from a specific regimen.
