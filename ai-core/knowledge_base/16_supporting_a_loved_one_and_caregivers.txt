TOPIC: Supporting a loved one and looking after yourself as a caregiver
Family members and friends often want to help but do not know how. Listening without rushing to fix things, asking what would be most useful today, and offering specific help such as driving to appointments, cooking a meal or sitting together can mean more than general advice. It is fine not to have the right words; simply staying present matters. Caregivers carry a heavy load too. Feeling tired, worried, resentful or guilty is common and does not mean you are failing. Sharing tasks among relatives, accepting help, keeping some time for sleep, meals and rest, and talking to a friend, counsellor or caregiver group all protect your own health. If you notice you are constantly exhausted, tearful or unable to cope, speak to a doctor or counsellor about your own needs as well.
