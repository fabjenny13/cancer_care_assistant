TOPIC: Trouble sleeping during cancer treatment
Sleep problems are very common during cancer and treatment. Worry, pain, hospital routines, night sweats and some medicines such as steroids can all disturb sleep. Helpful habits include keeping regular times for going to bed and waking, limiting long daytime naps to about 30 minutes, keeping the room dark, cool and quiet, and avoiding caffeine and screens in the hour before bed. A short wind-down routine such as a warm drink, gentle stretching or slow breathing signals the body to rest. If worries keep you awake, writing them down in a notebook by the bed can help to park them until morning. If you lie awake for more than about twenty minutes, getting up and doing something calm in low light can help. If poor sleep continues for more than a couple of weeks, tell your doctor, because there are safe ways to help.
