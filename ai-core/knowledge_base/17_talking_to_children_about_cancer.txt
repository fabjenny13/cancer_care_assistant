TOPIC: Talking to children about cancer
Children usually sense when something is wrong, and silence can make them imagine worse things. Experts generally advise honest, simple, age-appropriate explanations. Use the word cancer, explain that it is a illness in a part of the body, and that doctors are treating it. Reassure children that it is nobody's fault, that it is not catching, and that they will always be cared for. Younger children need short explanations and may show worry through play or behaviour, while teenagers may want more detail and some privacy. Tell them in advance about visible changes such as hair loss, and about changes to routine and who will pick them up. Invite questions, and it is fine to say you do not know the answer. Keeping school and regular routines as steady as possible helps. Teachers or a counsellor can offer extra support.
