TOPIC: Coping with anxiety before scans and results
SOURCE: Based on general guidance from NCI and WHO cancer support resources

Waiting for a scan or test result is one of the most anxiety-provoking parts of cancer care, often described by patients as "scanxiety." It's completely normal to feel your heart race, struggle to sleep, or replay worst-case scenarios in the days leading up to an appointment. This anxiety doesn't mean something is wrong with you emotionally — it's a natural response to genuine uncertainty about your health.

A few things can help in the moments leading up to a scan. Grounding techniques, like naming five things you can see and four things you can hear, can interrupt spiraling thoughts. Keeping the day of the scan as normal as possible, rather than clearing your whole schedule to worry, can also reduce the sense that the day is "all about the fear." Talking to someone — a friend, family member, or fellow patient who understands "scanxiety" — often helps more than trying to push the feeling away alone.

It's also worth remembering that this anxiety typically peaks right before the appointment and eases once you have an answer, whatever it is. If the anxiety is severe enough to disrupt sleep or daily functioning for extended periods, it's worth mentioning to your care team — they may be able to offer additional support.
