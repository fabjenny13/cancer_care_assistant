TOPIC: Radiation therapy basics
Radiation therapy uses focused high-energy beams to damage cancer cells so they cannot keep growing. External beam treatment is usually given in short daily sessions over several weeks, and each session itself is painless and takes only minutes. The person is not radioactive after external treatment. Side effects depend on the treated area and tend to build up gradually. Common ones are tiredness, skin that looks sunburned or feels dry and sore, and effects specific to the area, such as a sore throat or bowel changes. Skin care matters: use only the creams the team recommends, avoid rubbing or sun on the area, and wear loose soft clothing. Most side effects ease in the weeks after treatment ends. Anything that worries you, such as broken skin or trouble swallowing, is worth telling the radiation team early.
