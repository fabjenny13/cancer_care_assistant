TOPIC: Self-care basics during chemo and radiation
SOURCE: Based on general guidance from NCI and WHO cancer support resources

Self-care during active treatment looks different than it does at other times in life — it's less about indulgence and more about protecting your energy and mood through a physically demanding process. On low-energy days, self-care might simply mean allowing yourself to rest without guilt, rather than pushing through a normal routine.

Small, consistent habits tend to help more than big gestures. Gentle movement, like a short walk on days you feel able to, can improve mood even when a full workout isn't possible. Staying hydrated and eating small, manageable meals — rather than three large ones — is often easier on a treatment-affected appetite. Keeping a simple daily rhythm, even something small like a morning cup of tea at the same time each day, can provide a sense of stability when everything else feels unpredictable.

Emotional self-care matters just as much as physical care. This can mean setting boundaries around how much you discuss your diagnosis with people who drain rather than support you, or giving yourself permission to feel frustrated or sad without judging the feeling as wrong. There's no single "right" way to do self-care during treatment — the goal is finding what actually restores you, even in small amounts.
