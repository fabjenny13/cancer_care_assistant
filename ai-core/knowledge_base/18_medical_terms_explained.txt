TOPIC: Medical words explained in plain language
Cancer care comes with unfamiliar words, and learning them can reduce fear. A biopsy is a small sample of tissue examined under a microscope to find out whether cancer is present and what type. Staging describes how big the cancer is and whether it has spread, and is often given as a number from 1 to 4; the stage helps plan treatment. Metastasis means cancer that has spread from where it started to another part of the body. An oncologist is a cancer specialist. Adjuvant treatment is given after the main treatment, such as surgery, to lower the chance of the cancer coming back. Remission means signs of cancer have reduced or disappeared, which is not always the same as cured. Tumour markers are blood tests that are sometimes followed over time. Ask the team to explain any term you do not understand.
