TOPIC: Finding support groups and community resources
SOURCE: Based on general guidance from NCI and WHO cancer support resources

Support groups connect patients with others going through similar experiences, offering a kind of understanding that even the most caring friends or family members without cancer often can't fully provide. Groups exist in many formats — in-person, online, cancer-type-specific, age-specific (such as groups for young adults with cancer), or general.

Choosing the right fit matters more than joining any group at all. Some patients prefer structured groups led by a facilitator with a set format, while others prefer looser peer-support communities. It's reasonable to try a group once and decide it isn't the right fit, rather than assuming support groups in general aren't helpful.

Online communities can be particularly useful for patients with limited mobility, immune restrictions during active treatment, or those with rarer cancer types where local in-person groups may not exist. That said, online spaces vary widely in quality of moderation, so it's worth choosing established, moderated communities over unmoderated forums.

Hospital social workers or patient navigators are often the best starting point for finding vetted, cancer-specific groups, since they typically maintain lists of both local and online options and can match a patient to a group based on cancer type, stage, and personal preference.
